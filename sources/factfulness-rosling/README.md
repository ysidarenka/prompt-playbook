# Factfulness — Hans Rosling, Ola Rosling, Anna Rosling Rönnlund

**Book:** *Factfulness: Ten Reasons We're Wrong About the World — and Why Things Are Better Than You Think* (2018)
**More:** [Gapminder](https://www.gapminder.org/factfulness-book/), the foundation the authors started, publishes free data and tools.

## Key ideas (paraphrased)

- **Experts get basic facts wrong in predictable ways.** On the book's multiple-choice test about global trends, most people, including highly educated ones, score below random guessing. The cause is systematic bias, not missing knowledge.
- **Ten dramatic instincts** distort how we process information: gap, negativity, straight line, fear, size, generalization, destiny, single perspective, blame, and urgency. Each chapter ends with rules of thumb for controlling one of them. See the summary table in [Factfulness Check](../../frameworks/factfulness-ten-instincts.md).
- **The world isn't split into "developed" and "developing".** The book uses four income levels instead, and most people live in the middle levels.
- **Bad and better can both be true.** Separate the current level from the direction of change.
- **Factfulness is a habit, not a set of facts.** Facts go out of date, so the lasting skill is noticing when an instinct is being triggered and checking the data.
- **It applies beyond global development.** The last chapter applies the same ideas to education, business, journalism, and individual organizations, each of which has its own ignored facts.

## Ideas not yet extracted

- **Better and bad:** a prompt that splits any situation into "current level" and "direction of change" before drawing conclusions (negativity).
- **Question the category:** a prompt that breaks a segment ("our customers", "legacy systems") into smaller groups to test whether the label hides important differences (generalization).
- **Talk to Grandpa:** a prompt comparing how a practice or norm looked 20 years ago to show that "fixed" things move (destiny).
