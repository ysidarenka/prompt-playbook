# Stakeholder Simulation

## When to use

Before an important meeting, proposal, or approval: you want to hear objections from a customer, board member, executive, or approver while you can still fix them.

## Prompt

```text
Act as [STAKEHOLDER, described specifically, e.g. "a CFO at a mid-size lender who is skeptical of cloud spend and cares about predictable opex"].

Review [THE PROPOSAL / DECK / PLAN, or "the attached document"] from their point of view and tell me:
1. What they would like about it.
2. What they would push back on or not understand.
3. The top [3] changes that would make them say yes.

Then role-play the conversation: I'll present, and you respond as they would, resisting where they would. When I say "stop", give me feedback on what worked, what was weak, and what to change.
```

## Why it works

- A **specific description** of the stakeholder (role, priorities, worries) produces realistic objections. A generic "board member" produces generic ones.
- **Like / dislike / change** gives balanced feedback you can act on.
- The **role-play** lets you practice answers aloud, not just read a list of risks.
- Feedback **after** the role-play keeps the simulation from breaking character.

## Variations

- **Top questions:** "As this stakeholder, list the five questions you'd ask in a one-hour review, in priority order."
- **Investor lens on a message:** "As an investor, say where this communication reassures you and where it worries you, then rewrite it to reduce the worries without changing any facts."
